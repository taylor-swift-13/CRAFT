\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
%% ============================================================

Loop invariants turn unbounded executions into finite proofs, but a generated
response need not be wholly correct or wholly useless.  One incorrect clause
can invalidate the complete response even when its other clauses are valid and
useful.  CRAFT separates these two cases by decomposing each response into
clauses and verifying them jointly after pooling.  It can then compose
complementary valid clauses across responses, so even two responses that fail
individually may yield a correct invariant together.  Decompose preserves
useful parts of imperfect responses; compose combines complementary parts
across responses.  The filtered cross-response clause pool, rather than one
complete response, is therefore the natural inference object.

Whole-response training is mismatched to this inference procedure.  A failed
clause can obscure useful clauses from the same response, while response-level
rewards do not distinguish a complementary constraint from one repeated by
every peer.  More generally,
response-level RLVR can sharpen probability on already-supported answers
without expanding large-\(k\) coverage~\citep{yue2025rlcapacity}, potentially
reducing the diversity that composition needs.  Our central claim is therefore
that training feedback should align with the filtered clause pool constructed
at inference.  SFT uses accepted compositions as training targets, while RL
assigns credit according to each response's contribution to the same pool,
rather than treating every rollout as an independent final answer.

We study this claim under \emph{target-hidden loop verification}.  The model
sees the loop context but not the target assertions and postconditions \(\mathcal G\).
After the invariant set is fixed, the untouched source is restored and the
verifier checks both induction and every original target.  Hiding \(\mathcal G\)
prevents target copying and target-specific proof feedback, so generation and
training require a graded, target-independent strength signal.

At inference, \sam{} implements
\emph{rollout--decompose--pool--filter--compose}: given \(k\) responses, it
extracts their clauses, pools and filters them with syntax checks and Houdini,
and composes the survivors before restoring \(\mathcal G\).  compose@\(k\)
measures the verified rate of this composed result, preserving useful clauses
that pass@\(k\) discards; compose@1 is our primary optimization and reporting
metric.  Training uses the same filtered clause pool.  SFT distills an
accepted composition found through offline exploration into one response.  RL
rewards each response by the negative coverage of the clauses it contributes,
while a closed-form Shapley term across each GRPO
group~\citep{shao2024deepseekmath} discounts repeated contributions.  Both
stages reduce the number of inference-time samples needed to obtain the gains
of multi-response composition: SFT learns the composed result, and RL further
increases the probability that one rollout produces useful, nonredundant
clauses.
Both stages are self-generated: the only learned generator is
the small open-weight model being trained, while the program executor and
verifier provide every SFT acceptance decision and RL reward.  No external
teacher model generates targets, critiques responses, or supplies reward.

\tightparagraph{Contributions.}
This paper makes three contributions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item We introduce a rollout--decompose--pool--filter--compose inference
  framework for target-hidden invariant generation.  Decomposition exposes
  useful clauses inside responses that fail as a whole, pooling and filtering
  remove invalid or incompatible clauses, and composition combines
  complementary clauses across responses.  On Qwen3-8B, the framework raises
  one-response verification from 6.43\% pass@1 to 37.86\% compose@1, a
  31.43-point gain.
  \item We make SFT and RL use the same filtered clause pool constructed at
  inference: SFT distills an accepted multi-response composition into one
  response, while RL assigns credit according to each rollout's contribution
  to that pool.  On Qwen3-8B, SFT raises compose@1 to 63.90\%, and RL then
  reaches 69.23\% with only 4,992 records.  Both stages reduce the number of
  inference-time samples needed to obtain multi-response gains, and RL remains
  effective after SFT.  Under the same target-hidden, reasoning-off protocol,
  the resulting 8B model exceeds Claude Sonnet-4.6 at compose@1 by 12.26
  points; training uses no external teacher or reward model.
  \item Through controlled reward and sampler ablations, cross-model
  evaluation, and 832-program analysis, we demonstrate the importance of
  aligning training credit with the decomposed, pooled result used at
  inference.  Across all \(k\), full compositional credit improves over Binary
  by 49.88--54.69 compose points and over Whole-rollout by 39.06--51.33
  points; within the full reward, Shapley group credit adds 9.62--11.30 points
  over clause-level credit alone.
\end{itemize}
