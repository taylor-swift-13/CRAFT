\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
%% ============================================================

Loop invariants summarize an unbounded computation in a finite assertion.
For a loop \(\mathtt{while}\,(B)\,S\) with precondition
\(\phi_{\mathrm{pre}}\) and target
\(Q\), an invariant \(I\) must hold initially, be preserved by the loop body,
and retain enough information at the exit to prove \(Q\)
~\citep{floyd1967assigning,hoare1969axiomatic}:
\[
  \phi_{\mathrm{pre}}\Rightarrow I,\qquad
  \{I\wedge B\}\,S\,\{I\},\qquad
  I\wedge\neg B\Rightarrow Q.
\]
The first two conditions establish inductiveness, but they do not guarantee
usefulness: \(\mathit{true}\) is often inductive and proves nothing about the
loop exit.

Most invariant-synthesis systems are given \(Q\) during generation.
Template search, counterexample-guided methods, and recent neural systems can
therefore use the target or its failed proof obligations to guide the search
~\citep{gulwani2008program,gupta2009invgen,dillig2013inductive,
alur2013sygus,garg2014ice,garg2016learning,si2020code2inv,
kamath2023finding,cao2025clause2inv}.
This is appropriate when the verification target is already known, but it also
creates a shortcut: when \(Q\) is itself inductive, a generator can copy it
into the loop without recovering the relations that explain the program.

We study \emph{target-hidden loop verification}.  The generator sees the
precondition, loop guard, body, and in-scope variables, but all assertions and
postconditions are removed.  The original target \(Q\) is restored only after
the invariant set has been fixed.  Success still requires the complete
Frama-C/WP proof above, so hiding the target changes the information available
to generation without weakening the final criterion
(\S\ref{sec:overview}).

This setting removes the most direct training signal.  Inductiveness alone
cannot distinguish a strong loop summary from a tautology or a loose bound,
while using final proof success as a reward would expose the held-out target.
\sam{} instead executes the loop to collect reachable states and constructs
three kinds of synthetic negative examples without consulting \(Q\):
relational perturbations of observed traces, continuations after genuine loop
exits, and values outside sampled ranges.  Conservative checks remove
candidates that collide with observed executions, may be valid fresh entries,
or depend on unsupported state.

Training has two distinct data paths.  To construct synthetic SFT data,
multiple target-hidden responses are normalized, merged, optionally repaired,
and filtered with Houdini over Frama-C/WP.  The masked program is paired with
the resulting verified invariant set as a supervised example.  Reinforcement
learning uses the execution-derived positives and negatives described above to
reward clauses that are both inductive and behaviorally informative.  The same
candidate-processing steps are used at inference, where \(Q\) is restored only
for the final proof.

Our evaluation separates inference quality from the effect of reinforcement
learning.  On 832 integer C programs, five target-hidden responses with
candidate union and Houdini verify 622 programs (74.76\%), compared with 509
(61.18\%) for the strongest external baseline.  On the Qwen3-8B study,
reinforcement learning increases combine@10 from 52.40\% to 56.97\% and
reduces \(k_{95}\), the number of responses needed to reach 95\% of measured
combine@100, from 30 to 10.  A separate audit of 486,302 generated negative
states finds no hard pair-level collision under an expanded execution check.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
This paper makes three contributions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item We formulate target-hidden loop verification and an evaluation
  protocol in which \(Q\) is unavailable to generation, feedback, and reward,
  but remains the final acceptance criterion.
  \item We use one verified candidate-processing pipeline to synthesize SFT
  pairs, compute execution-based RL rewards, and perform inference.  The SFT
  targets are verified invariant sets; the separate RL signal uses
  conservatively filtered synthetic negatives.
  \item We evaluate inference, reinforcement learning, and negative-example
  quality separately, showing higher verification rates and substantially
  better rollout efficiency.
\end{itemize}
