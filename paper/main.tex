\begin{abstract}
Loop invariants are essential to program verification, reducing unbounded
loop executions to finite proof obligations.  Generating strong invariants is
especially difficult when verification targets are hidden: inductive validity
alone does not establish sufficient strength to prove the targets, while
generation lacks target guidance and training lacks target-specific proof
feedback.  Generated responses may contain incorrect clauses, and useful
clauses needed for a proof may be scattered across responses.  Inference can
exploit these responses through decomposition, filtering, and composition,
but whole-response training feedback may discard useful partial contributions,
creating a mismatch between training and inference.  We introduce CRAFT to
align compositional inference, supervision targets, and reinforcement-learning
rewards under \emph{target-hidden loop verification}.  At inference, CRAFT
decomposes responses into clauses, pools and jointly filters them, and composes
the survivors into a stronger invariant.  During training, target-independent
negative-trace coverage supplies a strength signal beyond inductive validity:
supervised fine-tuning (SFT) learns multi-response compositions accepted by
verification and coverage screening, while reinforcement learning (RL) rewards
each response for the individual and shared coverage contributions of its
clauses retained by the same pooled filtering procedure.  Training feedback thus reflects the useful
parts consumed at inference, without requiring each response to complete the
proof independently.  Target assertions and postconditions remain hidden
throughout generation and training and are restored only for final verification.
On 832 C programs, SFT raises Qwen3-8B's compose@1---the verification rate
after filtering a single response---from 37.86\% to 63.90\%, exceeding the
untrained model's 57.81\% compose@32.  Subsequent RL raises compose@1 to
69.23\%.  Reward ablations show that requiring whole-response correctness can
improve standalone success while degrading composition, supporting training
feedback aligned with the clauses retained and used by compositional inference.
\end{abstract}
