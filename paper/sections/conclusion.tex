\section{Conclusion and Limitations}\label{sec:conclusion}
%% ============================================================

Our results show that treating each response as an indivisible final answer
wastes useful clauses inside responses that fail as a whole.  By decomposing
responses and recombining their filtered clauses, CRAFT can exploit
complementary information across such responses.  This structure also
provides training signals aligned with inference: SFT learns compositions
found through offline exploration, while RL assigns credit according to each
rollout's contribution to the pooled result.  Both stages reduce the number
of inference-time samples needed to obtain multi-response gains, and RL
remains effective after SFT.

The reward ablations further show that improving standalone responses does
not necessarily improve their composition.  Whole-response credit raises
pass@1 but substantially reduces compose@32, whereas clause- and group-level
credit better preserve the partial correctness and cross-response
complementarity used at inference.

Our experiments focus on scalar-integer single-loop C programs, but the
underlying mechanism is not specific to loop invariants.  The same approach
can in principle apply when candidate solutions can be decomposed into
reusable components, these components can be reliably verified, and valid
components can be recombined.  These conditions allow the
decompose--filter--compose procedure to provide aligned SFT targets and RL
credit beyond the current setting.  Evaluating this transfer across other
program structures and problem domains remains future work
(Appendix~\ref{app:limitations}).
