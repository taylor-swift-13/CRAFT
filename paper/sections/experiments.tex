%% ============================================================
\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}
%% ============================================================

We organize the evaluation around three research questions.  RQ1 probes why
target-hidden RL must preserve useful exploration rather than optimize
soundness alone.  RQ2 evaluates the effectiveness and cost of the inference
framework against existing tools, with no LoopGym-trained policy involved.
RQ3 then tests whether framework-aligned RL improves the grouped
rollout--combine--Houdini objective even when standalone pass@\(k\) does not.
This separation follows prior invariant-synthesis evaluations that distinguish
candidate generation, candidate combination, and end-to-end tool performance
~\citep{kamath2023finding,cao2025clause2inv,wei2025invbench}.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp:setup}

\paragraph{Target-hidden acceptance.}
Before any model-facing or intermediate generation step, we remove ACSL
\texttt{assert} and \texttt{ensures} predicates, executable assertion calls,
and conventional error-label names.  Preconditions and executable loop
semantics remain visible.  After a method fixes its loop invariants, we insert
them into the untouched original program and restore the target.  A task
succeeds iff the source parses, Frama-C/WP proves establishment and preservation
for every retained invariant, and all restored assertion or postcondition goals
succeed.  Failures, timeouts, malformed outputs, and unsupported inputs count
as zero.

\paragraph{Verifier and model configurations.}
All final checks use Frama-C 32.1 (Germanium), WP, and Z3 4.15.4.  The RQ1
probe samples 100 target-hidden responses per task from the official
Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-14B, and Qwen3-30B-A3B checkpoints
~\citep{yang2025qwen3}, at temperature 1.0 with thinking disabled.  RQ2 fixes
\texttt{gpt-5-nano} across its LLM-backed rows; differences among the LoopGym
rows are therefore inference-only.  RQ3 compares Qwen3-8B with the
corresponding 8B-RL-Zero checkpoint under the same 100-rollout protocol.

\subsection{RQ1: Why Must the Reward Preserve Exploration?}
\label{sec:exp:probe}

\paragraph{Soundness is a degenerate reward.}
In target-hidden training, the exact target \(Q\) is unavailable, so the most
obvious verifier reward is whether a candidate satisfies initiation and
preservation.  This signal cannot distinguish a strong loop summary from
\texttt{true}, a loose bound, or another weak-but-sound invariant.  Under a
binary whole-response reward, these easy modes receive the same return as a
semantically strong invariant; once they dominate a rollout group, normalized
group advantages vanish and training has no incentive to discover stronger
relations.  A single malformed or non-inductive clause can also zero an
otherwise useful response, encouraging short and conservative outputs.

This failure is compounded by distribution sharpening.  The NeurIPS 2025 Best
Paper Runner-Up study of Yue et al. shows that RLVR can improve low-\(k\)
accuracy by concentrating probability mass on already rewarded reasoning paths
while narrowing the reasoning coverage exposed at large \(k\)
~\citep{yue2025rlcapacity}.  Such sharpening is particularly harmful here:
grouped inference depends on different rollouts contributing complementary
invariant clauses, not merely repeating the most likely sound clause.

\paragraph{Measuring complete and clause-level exploration.}
For each program, let \(c\) be the number of independently verifying responses
among \(n=100\).  Direct pass@\(k\) uses the unbiased estimator
\(1-\binom{n-c}{k}/\binom{n}{k}\) and asks whether some rollout independently
contains a complete proof.  Combine@\(k\) instead deduplicates all clauses in
the first \(k\) rollouts, applies iterative Houdini pruning, and checks whether
the surviving pool proves the restored target.  It therefore asks whether the
model has explored enough complementary proof ingredients for the inference
framework to assemble a proof.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/qwen_official_probe.pdf}
  \caption{Exploration probe over four official Qwen3 checkpoints.  Direct
  pass@\(k\) measures complete responses, whereas combine@\(k\) measures
  whether the accumulated clause pool contains a sufficient Houdini-surviving
  subset.  The combine curves change by at most 0.60 percentage points after
  \(k=30\).}
  \label{fig:qwen-probe}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:qwen-probe} exposes two limitations.  First, small models
rarely assemble a complete invariant set: direct pass@100 is 15.62\% for 4B,
compared with 33.29\% for 8B and 39.90\% for 14B.  Second, these weak complete
responses still contain useful pieces.  Union and Houdini raise 4B to 47.84\%
and 14B to 58.05\% at \(k=100\).  Combination thus recovers substantial value
from partial rollouts, but it does not erase the exploration gap: it cannot
invent a relation that the policy never emits.

Exploration also saturates early.  From combine@30 to combine@100, no model
gains more than 0.60 points, indicating that later responses contribute little
new proof-relevant coverage.  We summarize rollout efficiency by
\[
  k_{95}=\min\{k:C_\pi(k)\geq 0.95\,C_\pi(100)\},
\]
where \(C_\pi(k)\) is the verification rate after union and Houdini over \(k\)
policy samples.  A successful training objective should move this curve both
upward and leftward: raise the final exploration ceiling while reaching it
with fewer model generations.

\paragraph{Reward implication.}
LoopGym consequently treats inductiveness as a feasibility gate, not as the
complete reward.  Malformed lines are discarded and non-inductive clauses are
removed by the same Houdini operator used at inference; the remaining clauses
receive standalone strength credit, with extra positive hardness credit for
trace units rejected by few other rollouts.  Weak clauses add no coverage gain,
while conservative semantic duplicates receive a small explicit cost.  This
preserves the useful part of an imperfect response while rewarding exploration
without requiring a complete response-level proof.
Combination is therefore an exploration multiplier, not an exploration
substitute.

The reward sanity checks support this distinction without claiming a trained
policy result.  Across ten hand-written specifications, every gold clause
survives and the minimum gold base score is 0.996, with zero discrimination
violations.  A larger audit over Core-366 covers 486,302 candidate negative
states and finds no hard pair-level collision under the expanded sampling
check.

\subsection{RQ2: How Effective and Efficient Is the Inference Framework?}
\label{sec:exp:e2e}

\paragraph{Benchmark.}
We evaluate the inference framework on 832 integer C programs.  The
\emph{Core-366} subset contains 316 linear programs drawn from Code2Inv,
SyGuS, and SV-COMP and 50 nonlinear programs from LIPuS.  The remaining 466
programs are the integer fragment of Loopy's original 469-program corpus; three
floating-point programs are excluded because they fall outside our
scalar-integer predicate language.  Every method uses the same fixed manifest,
and unsupported inputs remain failures in the common denominator.

RQ2 evaluates inference, not model training.  All LoopGym variants use the
same \texttt{gpt-5-nano} checkpoint.  \textsc{Naive} uses one response with a
minimal prompt; \textsc{R1-NoH} changes only to the LoopGym prompt;
\textsc{R1-H} additionally applies Houdini; and \textsc{R5-H}/\textsc{R10-H}
union five/ten target-hidden rollouts before the same Houdini and final-WP
gate.  Re-rolling and refinement are disabled.

We compare with the native target-hidden pipelines of \textsc{AutoSpec}
~\citep{wen2024enchanting}, \textsc{SESpec}~\citep{yang2026sespec}, and
\textsc{Clause2Inv}~\citep{cao2025clause2inv}, plus Daikon 5.8.24
~\citep{daikon}.  Clause2Inv supports only the 366 programs with transition
VCs; its 466 unsupported inputs remain failures in the common denominator.

scaling the rollout budget indefinitely.

\subsection{RQ3: Does Framework-Aligned RL Improve Grouped Inference?}
\label{sec:exp:rlzero}

In its normal negative-coverage mode, the LoopGym reward is deliberately not a
binary verdict on an entire response.
Such a verdict discards all useful information when one clause is malformed or
non-inductive and encourages the weak conservative modes identified in RQ1.
Instead, syntax scrubbing and Houdini remove failed clauses, after which the
reward measures the standalone strength of the survivors and gives extra
positive credit to hard trace units rejected by few group peers.  RQ3 tests the
consequence of this alignment by comparing Qwen3-8B with 8B-RL-Zero under
identical inference settings.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/qwen_rlzero_probe.pdf}
  \caption{Framework-aligned RL improves grouped inference rather than
  standalone success.  RL-Zero slightly decreases direct pass@\(k\) (left)
  but consistently improves combine@\(k\) (right).  With ten rollouts,
  RL-Zero exceeds the base model's result with 100 rollouts.}
  \label{fig:qwen-rlzero}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:qwen-rlzero} shows a sharp divergence between the two metrics.
RL-Zero lowers direct pass@1 from 9.30\% to 4.88\% and leaves direct pass@100
nearly unchanged (33.29\% versus 32.93\%).  Judged only by standalone
verification, the trained policy would appear worse.  Under the deployed
pipeline, however, combine@10 rises from 52.40\% to 56.97\%, and combine@100
rises from 55.41\% to 58.77\%.  Ten RL-Zero rollouts already outperform 100
base-model rollouts, while measured \(k_{95}\) falls from 30 to 10.

The RL policy therefore moves the combine curve both upward and leftward: it
raises the attainable verification rate and reaches that rate with fewer model
generations.  This is the intended training outcome.  The objective does not
require every rollout to verify independently; it rewards the usable clauses
that survive pruning and cover difficult trace units, matching the diverse
clause supply consumed by rollout--combine--Houdini at deployment.
