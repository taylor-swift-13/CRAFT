%% ============================================================
\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
%% ============================================================

CRAFT couples an inference protocol that proves targets by
composing clauses across responses with a training pipeline that optimizes
that protocol without ever seeing the target.  Three design decisions
organize this section: (i)~\emph{target hiding}---generation, distillation,
and reward computation all operate on the masked loop, and the target is
restored only for the final proof (Section~\ref{sec:method:inference});
(ii)~\emph{the clause, not the response, is the credit unit} that survives
filtering and earns reward (Section~\ref{sec:method:core});
(iii)~\emph{training mirrors inference}---credit combines attributed
coverage with a smaller bonus for non-redundant coverage across a response
group, because inference unions that group
(Section~\ref{sec:method:reward}).  The guiding thesis is that because
inference composes, training should optimize composability: raising the
compose@\(k\) curve and reaching its large-pool performance with fewer
rollouts.

\subsection{Rollout--Decompose--Pool--Filter--Compose}
\label{sec:method:core}

For a target-masked loop \(\mathcal L\), the model emits ACSL loop-invariant
annotations~\citep{acsl} in a raw response \(Y_i\).  Parsing extracts each
loop-invariant annotation as one clause; normalization turns
\(Y_i\) into the clause sequence
\(A_i=\langle a_{i1},\ldots,a_{im_i}\rangle\).  We cap it at \(K\) clauses
(\(K{=}20\); Appendix~\ref{app:constants}),
\(\bar A_i=\langle a_{i1},\ldots,a_{i,\min(m_i,K)}\rangle\), and discard
the rest.  Extraction makes the annotation, rather than the complete
response, the unit that can survive filtering and earn credit.  The
pipeline has five steps: \emph{rollout} samples responses, \emph{decompose}
extracts their clauses, \emph{pool} unions the clauses across responses,
\emph{filter} removes syntax failures and non-inductive clauses from the
pool, and \emph{compose} conjoins the survivors into one invariant.  We write
\(\operatorname{set}(\bar A_i)\) for the underlying clause set when applying
set operations.

Given \(n\) responses, the pipeline unions their clauses,
\[
  C_n^{\mathrm{pool}}=
  \bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{set}(\bar A_i),
\]
then iteratively removes syntax failures and applies Houdini to delete clauses
whose establishment or preservation obligations fail
~\citep{flanagan2001houdini}.  We write the resulting inductive subset as
\(\mathsf{Filter}_{\mathcal L}(C_n^{\mathrm{pool}})\).  Conjoining valid clauses preserves
validity and can compose a bound from one response with a relation from
another; filtering keeps such clauses from otherwise imperfect responses
but cannot invent a missing relation.  Algorithm~\ref{alg:rcf} computes
the implemented \(\mathsf{Filter}_{\mathcal L}\) of
Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries}, whose ideal fixed point is
\(\mathsf H_{\mathcal L}(C)\).  The algorithm is shared by
SFT synthesis, RL reward filtering, and inference; RL additionally retains
clause ownership for credit assignment.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Rollout--decompose--pool--filter--compose}
\label{alg:rcf}
\begin{algorithmic}
\alginput{masked loop \(\mathcal L\), normalized clause sequences \(\bar A_{1:n}\)}
\algoutput{filtered clause set \(C\)}
\algline \(C\leftarrow\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{set}(\bar A_i)\)
  \cmnt{decompose, pool}\\
\algline \(C\leftarrow\operatorname{ConservativeDedup}(C)\)\cmnt{filter: dedup}\\
\algline \kw{repeat}\cmnt{filter: syntax}\\
\algline \algind \(C_{\mathrm{parse}}\leftarrow
  \operatorname{ParserErrors}(\mathcal L,C)\);
  \(C\leftarrow C\setminus C_{\mathrm{parse}}\)\\
\algline \kw{until} \(C_{\mathrm{parse}}=\varnothing\) or \(C=\varnothing\)\\
\algline \kw{repeat}\cmnt{filter: Houdini}\\
\algline \algind \(C_{\mathrm{drop}}\leftarrow\{c\in C:
  \neg\widehat{\operatorname{WP}}_{\mathrm{est,pres}}(\mathcal L,C,c)\}\);
  \(C\leftarrow C\setminus C_{\mathrm{drop}}\)\\
\algline \kw{until} \(C_{\mathrm{drop}}=\varnothing\) or \(C=\varnothing\)\\
\algline \kw{return} \(C\)\cmnt{compose: \(I_C=\bigwedge_{c\in C}c\)}\\
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Here the ideal predicate
\(\operatorname{WP}^{\star}_{\mathrm{est,pres}}(\mathcal L,C,c)\) is true
exactly when the establishment and preservation obligations of clause \(c\)
under the current context \(C\) are logically valid.  Algorithm~\ref{alg:rcf}
instead uses its implemented, resource-bounded approximation
\(\widehat{\operatorname{WP}}_{\mathrm{est,pres}}\): Frama-C/WP generates
the obligations, Alt-Ergo and Z3 attempt
them within the configured timeout, and the predicate returns true only when
all required obligations are proved.  An unknown result, prover failure, or
timeout is treated as false and the clause is conservatively deleted.  Thus
Theorem~\ref{thm:houdini-greatest} concerns
\(\operatorname{WP}^{\star}_{\mathrm{est,pres}}\), whereas experiments use
the implemented predicate.

The following statements formalize the composition used by the pipeline; all proofs
are deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:composition-proofs}.

\begin{proposition}[Closure and strengthening under conjunction]
\label{prop:conjunctive-closure}
Let \(I_1,\ldots,I_m\) be valid invariants of the same loop, for finite
\(m\ge 1\).  Then \(I=\bigwedge_{j=1}^{m}I_j\) is valid and implies every
\(I_j\), i.e., \(I\preceq I_j\).  Moreover, \(I\prec I_j\) exactly when
\(I_j\not\Rightarrow\bigwedge_{i\ne j}I_i\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Mutually supported composition]
\label{prop:mutual-support}
Let \(C=\{c_1,\ldots,c_m\}\) be any finite set of candidate clauses.  The
conjunction \(I_C\) is a valid invariant
whenever

\[
  P\Rightarrow I_C,
  \qquad
  \forall j,\ \{I_C\wedge B\}\,S\,\{c_j\}.
\]

Individual obligations
\(\{c_j\wedge B\}\,S\,\{c_j\}\) need not hold: preservation may be supplied
by any number of companion clauses in \(C\).  Mutual support cannot, however,
repair a clause that fails establishment from \(P\).  The resulting valid
invariant satisfies \(I_C\preceq c_j\), with \(I_C\prec c_j\) whenever
\(c_j\not\Rightarrow I_{C\setminus\{c_j\}}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Candidate-relative optimality of ideal Houdini]
\label{thm:houdini-greatest}
For a finite parser-admitted pool \(C\), replace the implemented
\(\widehat{\operatorname{WP}}_{\mathrm{est,pres}}\) in
Algorithm~\ref{alg:rcf} by the exact
\(\operatorname{WP}^{\star}_{\mathrm{est,pres}}\).
Its fixed point \(\mathsf H_{\mathcal L}(C)\) is jointly inductive, and every
jointly inductive subset \(C'\subseteq C\) satisfies
\(C'\subseteq\mathsf H_{\mathcal L}(C)\).  Consequently,
\(I_{\mathsf H_{\mathcal L}(C)}\preceq I_{C'}\): the result is the strongest
conjunction expressible as a subset of \(C\).
\end{theorem}

This is the classic Houdini greatest-fixpoint
property~\citep{flanagan2001houdini}, restated in our clause notation.  In
practice the per-obligation prover budget (Section~\ref{sec:exp:setup})
makes this oracle approximate.

For RL scoring, after the rollout clauses have been pooled, the filter also
removes clauses contradicted by observed reachable states \(\mathcal E\)
before running Houdini:
\[
  \mathsf{PF}_{\mathcal E}(C)
  =\{c\in C:\forall p\in\mathcal E,\ p\models c\}.
\]
This positive filter is a cheap refutation step used only for RL scoring.
Appendix~\ref{app:constants} gives normalization and filtering details.

\subsection{Inference Objective}
\label{sec:method:inference}

At inference, the pipeline samples \(k\) masked responses
\(Y_{1:k}\sim\pi(\cdot\mid\mathcal L)\) and produces
\[
  C_k^{\mathrm{pool}}=
  \bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\operatorname{set}(\bar A_i),\qquad
  C_k=\mathsf{Filter}_{\mathcal L}(C_k^{\mathrm{pool}}),\qquad
  I_k=I_{C_k}.
\]
Only then is \(Q\) restored.  The inference objective is the expected
target-utility indicator,
\[
  \max_\pi\ \mathbb E_{Y_{1:k}\sim\pi(\cdot\mid\mathcal L)}\,
  \mathbf 1[\operatorname{Verify}(\mathcal L,I_k,Q)],
\]
where \(\operatorname{Verify}\) requires establishment, preservation, and every
restored target under the path condition at its original location, as defined
in Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries}; for an in-body assertion the obligation is
\(\{I_k\wedge B\}S_{<\ell}\{q_\ell\}\), and for one post-loop target it is
\(I_k\wedge\neg B\Rightarrow q_\ell\).
Its empirical counterpart is compose@\(k\), which can succeed even when no
standalone response is sufficient.  The training sections below therefore
optimize a target-hidden surrogate for this curve.

\subsection{Target-Independent Strength Examples}
\label{sec:method:sampler}

To grade inductive sets without \(Q\), we execute a deterministic,
precondition-checked input sweep and record the observed loop-head states
\(\mathcal E\subseteq\operatorname{Reach}(\mathcal L)\).  From these executions we construct two structured
negative-example families: local one- or two-variable perturbations that
break relations, and continuations of the real body after a witnessed loop
exit.  Cleaning removes observed reachable states, possible fresh entries,
duplicates, and candidates involving unsupported state.  The structured
sampler fills any unused slots with uniformly sampled local perturbations;
the Random control uses the same generator for all slots.  Thus both modes
receive a matched budget of 60 negative traces.  Figure~\ref{fig:negative-sampling},
Algorithm~\ref{alg:negative-sampling}, and exact budgets appear in
Appendix~\ref{app:negative-sampling}.

Let \(\mathcal N\) be the retained negative traces.  For a trace
\(\nu=\langle s_0,\ldots,s_T\rangle\), define
\(\nu\models c\) iff \(s_t\models c\) for every \(t\in\{0,\ldots,T\}\).
For a retained clause set \(C\),
\[
  \operatorname{rej}(C)
  =\{\nu\in\mathcal N:\exists c\in C,\ \nu\not\models c\}.
\]
Thus a trace is rejected when at least one clause in \(C\) is false at some
recorded state; singleton traces recover ordinary state rejection.  All
coverage below is therefore \emph{trace-level empirical coverage}: each
retained trace has weight one regardless of its number of states.  In
particular, regrouping the same states into a different collection of traces
can change the reported coverage; the trace construction and grouping are
fixed by Algorithm~\ref{alg:negative-sampling}.

\subsection{Distilling Multi-Response Search}
\label{sec:method:sft}

SFT targets are built by the same rollout--decompose--pool--filter--compose
loop that inference runs, driven by the untrained policy and accepted by the
reward.  For each masked loop \(\mathcal L\) in the training corpus
\(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{train}}\), round \(t\) samples a group of
\(G_{\mathrm{SFT}}{=}16\) target-hidden responses from the base model
(Qwen3-8B), adds their clauses to the pool, and filters the union to an
inductive set,
\[
  C^{(t)}_{\mathcal L}
  =\mathsf{Filter}_{\mathcal L}\!\Bigl(C^{(t-1)}_{\mathcal L}\cup
    \bigcup_{i=1}^{G_{\mathrm{SFT}}}\operatorname{set}(\bar A^{(t)}_i)\Bigr),
  \qquad C^{(0)}_{\mathcal L}=\varnothing.
\]
The filtered candidate is scored on the negative traces of
Section~\ref{sec:method:sampler}.  For \(\mathcal N\ne\varnothing\), define
\[
  \operatorname{cov}(C)
  =\frac{|\operatorname{rej}(C)|}{|\mathcal N|}.
\]
When \(\mathcal N=\varnothing\), this ratio is undefined and the example is
marked unscorable: it is not admitted to the SFT corpus.  (RL uses the binary
fallback specified in Section~\ref{sec:method:reward}.)
The search stops when
\(\operatorname{cov}(C^{(t)}_{\mathcal L})\ge\tau\), where the experiments
use \(\tau=0.5\), with
\(C^{\mathrm{SFT}}_{\mathcal L}=C^{(t)}_{\mathcal L}\); otherwise another
group is sampled and added to the accumulated pool, for at most \(T_{\mathrm{SFT}}{=}5\)
rounds (80 responses).  Loops that do not reach \(\tau\) within this budget
are dropped.  Here coverage is an acceptance criterion for an already
filtered, composed SFT target; Section~\ref{sec:method:reward} instead assigns
clause-level credit within imperfect RL responses.

Before serialization, we remove normalized duplicates, tautologies, and
clauses implied by other retained atomic clauses, while retaining all
remaining clauses to preserve coverage.  Every accepted set is serialized
as one SFT target:
\[
  \mathcal D_{\mathrm{SFT}}
  =\{(\mathcal L,\operatorname{serialize}(C_{\mathcal L}^{\mathrm{SFT}})):
      \mathcal L\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{train}},\
      \mathcal N_{\mathcal L}\ne\varnothing,\
      \operatorname{cov}(C_{\mathcal L}^{\mathrm{SFT}})\ge\tau\}.
\]
Neither input nor target contains \(Q\): SFT serializes a reward-accepted,
Houdini-retained multi-response search result as one response.

\subsection{Clause-Decomposed, Group-Compositional Reward}
\label{sec:method:reward}

The reward design responds directly to the sharpening tendency described in
Section~\ref{sec:intro}.  Group-relative optimization with a whole-response
reward concentrates probability mass on a few high-reward responses, eroding
the clause diversity that the pipeline composes at inference.  We therefore decompose
credit \emph{within} each rollout---attributed survivors earn reward even when
siblings fail---and re-allocate credit \emph{across} the group by each
rollout's expected marginal contribution in a fixed, group-conditioned
coverage game.  This second term is a bonus for coverage supplied by fewer
peers; the larger first term still rewards ordinary attributed coverage,
including repeated coverage.

For rollout \(i\), normalize its clauses without making any proof decision.
Then pool the entire group \(\mathcal G=\{1,\ldots,G\}\) and run the complete
RL filter exactly once:
\[
  C_i^{\mathrm{raw}}=\operatorname{set}(\bar A_i),\qquad
  C_{\mathcal G}^{\mathrm{pool}}=
  \operatorname{ConservativeDedup}\!\left(
    \bigcup_{j=1}^{G}C_j^{\mathrm{raw}}\right),
\]
\[
  C_{\mathcal G}^{\mathrm{surv}}=
  \mathsf{Filter}_{\mathcal L}\!\left(
    \mathsf{PF}_{\mathcal E}(C_{\mathcal G}^{\mathrm{pool}})\right).
\]
Before pooled de-duplication, each clause carries a
\emph{canonical normalization key} \(\operatorname{nkey}(c)\).  The key
identifies spellings equated by the finite normalization rules in
Appendix~\ref{app:grpo-details}; it is not a decision procedure for logical
equivalence.  Define the provenance map
\[
  \Omega(\kappa)=\{i:\exists c\in C_i^{\mathrm{raw}},\
  \operatorname{nkey}(c)=\kappa\}.
\]
We assign each pooled survivor back to every rollout in its owner set,
\[
  C_i^{\mathrm{attr}}(\mathcal G)
  =\{c\in C_{\mathcal G}^{\mathrm{surv}}:
      i\in \Omega(\operatorname{nkey}(c))\},
  \qquad R_i(\mathcal G)=
  \operatorname{rej}(C_i^{\mathrm{attr}}(\mathcal G)).
\]
This projection implements \emph{intra-rollout decomposition}: malformed or
non-inductive clauses are removed without erasing useful clauses in the same
response, while mutually supporting clauses from different responses are
filtered under the same pooled context used at inference
(Appendix~\ref{app:houdini-order}).  When retained negatives exist, the reward adds no bonus merely for
valid syntax or whole-response validity.  The subset receives the dense,
target-independent strength score
\[
  \operatorname{cov}_i(\mathcal G)=
  \frac{|R_i(\mathcal G)|}{|\mathcal N|}.
\]
This and the following allocation apply when \(\mathcal N\neq\varnothing\);
when the sampler retains none, we use the binary fallback in
Appendix~\ref{app:grpo-details}.

Clause coverage alone, however, rewards a complementary discovery exactly like
one repeated by every peer.  Conditioning on the full-group survivor set, we
define the fixed coverage game
\[
  v_{\mathcal G}(\mathcal S)=
  \frac{\left|\bigcup_{i\in\mathcal S}R_i(\mathcal G)\right|}
       {|\mathcal N|},
  \qquad \mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal G.
\]
Let
\(f_{\mathcal G}(\nu)=\sum_{j=1}^{G}
\mathbf 1[\nu\in R_j(\mathcal G)]\) count how many attributed rollout sets
reject trace \(\nu\).  The Shapley value of rollout \(i\) in this fixed
coverage game has the closed form~
\citep{shapley1953value} (proof in Appendix~\ref{app:composition-proofs})
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:shapley-credit}
  \phi_i(\mathcal G)=\frac{1}{|\mathcal N|}
  \sum_{\nu\in R_i(\mathcal G)}\frac{1}{f_{\mathcal G}(\nu)},
  \qquad
  \sum_{i=1}^{G}\phi_i(\mathcal G)
  =v_{\mathcal G}(\mathcal G)
  =\frac{|\operatorname{rej}(C_{\mathcal G}^{\mathrm{surv}})|}
         {|\mathcal N|}.
\end{equation}
Thus a unique rejection receives full group credit, whereas
\(f_{\mathcal G}(\nu)\) redundant rejections split that group-credit unit.
For this fixed game, the Shapley allocation is the unique credit rule
satisfying efficiency, symmetry, linearity, and the null-player
axiom~
\citep{shapley1953value}.  It does not re-run filtering for any subcoalition
and therefore is not the Shapley value of the complete
pool--filter--prove system.  It implements a limited inter-rollout bonus: the
same response earns less \emph{group credit} when its verified constraints
are already supplied by peers, while its ordinary coverage credit is
unchanged.

The reward combines the two credits,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:generation-reward}
  r_i(\mathcal G)=w_c\,\operatorname{cov}_i(\mathcal G)
  +w_g\phi_i(\mathcal G),
\end{equation}
where \((w_c,w_g)=(1.0,0.3)\).  We call the first term the \emph{clause
credit} and the second the \emph{group (Shapley) credit};
Section~\ref{sec:exp:ablations} ablates each.  GRPO normalizes these
group-conditioned rewards within the sampled response group.
Because \(w_c>w_g\), this is a coverage reward with a complementarity bonus.
Repeatedly covered traces
still contribute fully to \(\operatorname{cov}_i\).
If an attributed survivor rejects no
negative trace, it contributes zero to both terms.  Such a clause may still
be essential support that lets another clause pass joint inductiveness, so
this reward does not fully measure the clause's value to the composed proof.
The response cap discards
later clauses before filtering rather than penalizing them.
Each training step samples a group of \(G{=}8\) responses per masked loop,
scores them by Equation~\eqref{eq:generation-reward}, and applies one GRPO
update; this training-time group is distinct from the inference sweep over
\(k\) (Section~\ref{sec:method:inference}).
Appendix~\ref{app:grpo-details} gives the standard
policy objective, clause normalization, and fallback behavior.
