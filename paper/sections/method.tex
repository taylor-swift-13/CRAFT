%% ============================================================
\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
%% ============================================================

CRAFT couples an inference protocol that proves targets by
composing clauses across responses with a training pipeline that optimizes
that protocol without ever seeing the target.  Three design decisions
organize this section: (i)~\emph{target hiding}---generation, distillation,
and reward computation all operate on the masked loop, and the target is
restored only for the final proof (Section~\ref{sec:method:inference});
(ii)~\emph{the clause, not the response, is the credit unit} that survives
filtering and earns reward (Section~\ref{sec:method:core});
(iii)~\emph{training mirrors inference}---credit rewards complementary
coverage across a response group, because inference unions that group
(Section~\ref{sec:method:reward}).  The guiding thesis is that because
inference composes, training should optimize composability: raising the
compose@$k$ curve and reaching its large-pool performance with fewer
rollouts.

\subsection{Rollout--Decompose--Filter--Compose}
\label{sec:method:core}

For a target-masked loop \(\mathcal L\), the model emits ACSL loop-invariant
annotations~\citep{acsl}.  Parsing extracts each
\texttt{loop invariant ...;} annotation as one clause; normalization turns
response \(i\) into the clause sequence
\(A_i=\langle a_{i1},\ldots,a_{im_i}\rangle\).  We cap it at \(K\) clauses
(\(K{=}20\); Appendix~\ref{app:constants}),
\(\bar A_i=\langle a_{i1},\ldots,a_{i,\min(m_i,K)}\rangle\), with overflow
\(o(A_i)=\max(0,m_i-K)\).  Extraction makes the annotation, rather than the complete
response, the unit that can survive filtering and earn credit.  We write
\(\operatorname{set}(\bar A_i)\) for the underlying clause set when applying
set operations.

Given \(G\) responses, the pipeline unions their clauses,
\[
  C_G^{\mathrm{pool}}=
  \bigcup_{i=1}^{G}\operatorname{set}(\bar A_i),
\]
then iteratively removes syntax failures and applies Houdini to delete clauses
whose establishment or preservation obligations fail
~\citep{flanagan2001houdini}.  We write the resulting inductive subset as
\(\mathsf{Filter}_{\mathcal L}(C_G^{\mathrm{pool}})\).  Conjoining valid clauses preserves
validity and can compose a bound from one response with a relation from
another; filtering keeps such clauses from otherwise imperfect responses
but cannot invent a missing relation.  Algorithm~\ref{alg:rcf} computes
\(\mathsf{Filter}_{\mathcal L}\); we write its fixed point as
\(\mathsf H_{\mathcal L}(C)\) and the conjunction of a clause set \(C\) as
\(I_C=\bigwedge_{c\in C}c\).  The algorithm is shared by
SFT synthesis and inference.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Rollout--decompose--filter--compose}
\label{alg:rcf}
\begin{algorithmic}
\alginput{masked loop \(\mathcal L\), parsed clause sequences \(A_{1:G}\), cap \(K\)}
\algoutput{filtered clause set \(C\)}
\algline \(C\leftarrow\bigcup_i\operatorname{set}\!\left(
  \operatorname{Cap}_K(\operatorname{Normalize}(A_i))\right)\)\\
\algline \(C\leftarrow\operatorname{ConservativeDedup}(C)\)\\
\algline \kw{repeat}\\
\algline \algind \(C_{\mathrm{parse}}\leftarrow
  \operatorname{ParserErrors}(\mathcal L,C)\);
  \(C\leftarrow C\setminus C_{\mathrm{parse}}\)\\
\algline \kw{until} \(C_{\mathrm{parse}}=\varnothing\) or \(C=\varnothing\)\\
\algline \kw{repeat}\\
\algline \algind \(C_{\mathrm{drop}}\leftarrow\{c\in C:
  \neg\operatorname{WP}_{\mathrm{est,pres}}(\mathcal L,C,c)\}\);
  \(C\leftarrow C\setminus C_{\mathrm{drop}}\)\\
\algline \kw{until} \(C_{\mathrm{drop}}=\varnothing\) or \(C=\varnothing\)\\
\algline \kw{return} \(C\)\\
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The following statements formalize the composition used by the pipeline; all proofs
are deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:composition-proofs}.

\begin{proposition}[Closure and strengthening under conjunction]
\label{prop:conjunctive-closure}
Let \(I_1,\ldots,I_m\) be valid invariants of the same loop, for finite
\(m\ge 1\).  Then \(I=\bigwedge_{j=1}^{m}I_j\) is valid and implies every
\(I_j\), i.e., \(I\preceq I_j\).  Moreover, \(I\prec I_j\) exactly when
\(I_j\not\Rightarrow\bigwedge_{\ell\ne j}I_\ell\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Mutually supported composition]
\label{prop:mutual-support}
Let \(C=\{c_1,\ldots,c_m\}\) be any finite set of candidate clauses.  The
conjunction \(I_C\) is a valid invariant
whenever

\[
  P\Rightarrow I_C,
  \qquad
  \forall j,\ \{I_C\wedge B\}\,S\,\{c_j\}.
\]

Individual obligations
\(\{c_j\wedge B\}\,S\,\{c_j\}\) need not hold: preservation may be supplied
by any number of companion clauses in \(C\).  Mutual support cannot, however,
repair a clause that fails establishment from \(P\).  The resulting valid
invariant satisfies \(I_C\preceq c_j\), with \(I_C\prec c_j\) whenever
\(c_j\not\Rightarrow I_{C\setminus\{c_j\}}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Candidate-relative optimality of ideal Houdini]
\label{thm:houdini-greatest}
For a finite parser-admitted pool \(C\), assume exact decisions for all
establishment and preservation obligations in Algorithm~\ref{alg:rcf}.
Its fixed point \(\mathsf H_{\mathcal L}(C)\) is jointly inductive, and every
jointly inductive subset \(C'\subseteq C\) satisfies
\(C'\subseteq\mathsf H_{\mathcal L}(C)\).  Consequently,
\(I_{\mathsf H_{\mathcal L}(C)}\preceq I_{C'}\): the result is the strongest
conjunction expressible as a subset of \(C\).
\end{theorem}

This is the classic Houdini greatest-fixpoint
property~\citep{flanagan2001houdini}, restated in our clause notation.  In
practice the per-obligation prover budget (Section~\ref{sec:exp:setup})
makes this oracle approximate.

RL scoring additionally removes clauses contradicted by observed reachable
states \(\mathcal P\) before Houdini:
\[
  \mathsf{PF}_{\mathcal P}(C)
  =\{c\in C:\forall p\in\mathcal P,\ p\models c\}.
\]
This positive filter is a cheap refutation step used only for RL scoring.
Appendix~\ref{app:constants} gives normalization and filtering details.

\subsection{Inference Objective}
\label{sec:method:inference}

At inference, the pipeline samples \(k\) masked responses and produces
\[
  C_k^{\mathrm{pool}}=
  \bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\operatorname{set}(\bar A_i),\qquad
  C_k=\mathsf{Filter}_{\mathcal L}(C_k^{\mathrm{pool}}),\qquad
  I_k=I_{C_k}.
\]
Only then is \(Q\) restored.  The target utility and inference objective are
\[
  U_Q(A_{1:k})=\mathbf 1[\operatorname{Verify}(\mathcal L,I_k,Q)],
  \qquad
  \max_\pi\ \mathbb E_{A_{1:k}\sim\pi(\cdot\mid\mathcal L)}[U_Q(A_{1:k})].
\]
Here \(\operatorname{Verify}\) requires establishment, preservation, and every
restored target at its original location; for one post-loop target, its last
obligation is \(I_k\wedge\neg B\Rightarrow q\).
Its empirical counterpart is compose@\(k\), which can succeed even when no
standalone response is sufficient.  The training sections below therefore
optimize a target-hidden surrogate for this curve.

\subsection{Target-Independent Strength Examples}
\label{sec:method:sampler}

To grade inductive sets without \(Q\), we execute a deterministic,
precondition-checked input sweep and record reachable loop-head states
\(\mathcal P\).  From these executions we construct two negative-example
families: local one- or two-variable perturbations that break relations, and
continuations of the real body after a witnessed loop exit.  Cleaning is
deliberately conservative---any candidate that executions cannot rule out is
dropped: it removes observed reachable states, possible fresh entries,
duplicates, and candidates involving unsupported state.  Unsupported or
incomplete executions disable the affected family.  Figure~\ref{fig:negative-sampling}, Algorithm~\ref{alg:negative-sampling},
and exact budgets appear in Appendix~\ref{app:negative-sampling}.

Let \(\mathcal N\) be the retained negative traces.  For a retained clause set
\(C\), \(\operatorname{rej}(C)\subseteq\mathcal N\) contains each trace on which at
least one clause in \(C\) is false at some recorded state; singleton traces
recover ordinary state rejection.

\subsection{Distilling Multi-Response Search}
\label{sec:method:sft}

For each masked loop \(\mathcal L\) in the training corpus
\(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{train}}\), let
\(C_{\mathcal L}^{\mathrm{pool}}\) be a sampled response-group union and
\(C_{\mathcal L}^{\mathrm{sft}}=\mathsf{Filter}_{\mathcal L}
(C_{\mathcal L}^{\mathrm{pool}})\).  We
serialize every nonempty result as one SFT target:
\[
  \mathcal D_{\mathrm{SFT}}
  =\{(\mathcal L,\operatorname{serialize}(C_{\mathcal L}^{\mathrm{sft}})):
      \mathcal L\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{train}},\
      C_{\mathcal L}^{\mathrm{sft}}\neq\varnothing\}.
\]
Neither input nor target contains \(Q\): SFT serializes a Houdini-retained
multi-response search result as one response.

\subsection{Clause-Decomposed, Group-Compositional Reward}
\label{sec:method:reward}

The reward design responds directly to the sharpening tendency described in
Section~\ref{sec:intro}.  Group-relative optimization with a whole-response
reward concentrates probability mass on a few high-reward responses, eroding
the clause diversity that the pipeline composes at inference.  We therefore decompose
credit \emph{within} each rollout---surviving clauses earn reward even when
siblings fail---and re-allocate credit \emph{across} the group by each
rollout's marginal contribution to pooled coverage, paying the policy for
expanding the joint clause pool rather than for reproducing its most common
clauses.

For rollout \(i\), define its verifier-retained subset and rejected negatives
as
\[
  C_i^{\mathrm{pf}}=
  \mathsf{PF}_{\mathcal P}(\operatorname{set}(\bar A_i)),\qquad
  C_i^{\mathrm{train}}=\mathsf{Filter}_{\mathcal L}(C_i^{\mathrm{pf}}),
  \qquad R_i=\operatorname{rej}(C_i^{\mathrm{train}}).
\]
This projection implements \emph{intra-rollout decomposition}: malformed or
non-inductive clauses are removed without erasing useful clauses in the same
response.  When retained negatives exist, the reward adds no bonus merely for
valid syntax or whole-response validity.  The subset receives the dense,
target-independent strength score
\[
  \operatorname{cov}(A_i)=\frac{|R_i|}{|\mathcal N|}.
\]
This and the following allocation apply when \(\mathcal N\neq\varnothing\);
when the sampler retains none, we use the binary fallback in
Appendix~\ref{app:grpo-details}.

Independent coverage, however, rewards a complementary discovery exactly like
one repeated by every peer.  We therefore allocate credit for the union
\(\bigcup_i R_i\) across the rollout group \(\mathcal G=\{1,\ldots,G\}\) as
a coverage game.  Let
\(f(n)=\sum_{j=1}^{G}\mathbf 1[n\in R_j]\) count how many peers reject
trace \(n\).  The Shapley value of rollout
\(i\) has the closed form~
\citep{shapley1953value} (proof in Appendix~\ref{app:composition-proofs})
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:shapley-credit}
  \phi_i=\frac{1}{|\mathcal N|}
  \sum_{n\in R_i}\frac{1}{f(n)},
  \qquad
  \sum_{i=1}^{G}\phi_i
  =\frac{|\bigcup_iR_i|}{|\mathcal N|}.
\end{equation}
Thus a unique rejection receives full credit, whereas \(f(n)\) redundant
rejections split it.  The Shapley allocation is the unique credit rule
satisfying efficiency, symmetry, linearity, and the null-player
axiom~
\citep{shapley1953value}.  It implements \emph{inter-rollout composition}:
the same response earns less group credit when its verified constraints are
already supplied by peers.

With the within-response duplicate count
\(d_{\mathrm{dup}}(A_i)=|\bar A_i|-|\operatorname{dedup}(\bar A_i)|\)
(clauses identical after normalization), the reward is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:generation-reward}
  r_i=w_c\,\operatorname{cov}(A_i)+w_g\phi_i
      -w_d d_{\mathrm{dup}}(A_i)-w_o o(A_i),
\end{equation}
where \((w_c,w_g,w_d,w_o)=(1.0,0.3,0.02,0.05)\);
Section~\ref{sec:exp:ablations} ablates each term.  GRPO normalizes these
group-conditioned rewards within the sampled response group.
Each training step samples a group of \(G\) responses per masked loop,
scores them by Equation~\eqref{eq:generation-reward}, and applies one GRPO
update; this training-time group is distinct from the inference sweep over
\(k\) (Section~\ref{sec:method:inference}).
Appendix~\ref{app:grpo-details} gives the standard
policy objective, duplicate normalization, and fallback behavior.
