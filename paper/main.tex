\begin{abstract}
Loop-invariant generators are commonly given the assertion or postcondition
that their output must prove.  This can simplify generation to copying or
specializing to the target rather than recovering the behavior of the loop.
We study \emph{target-hidden loop verification}: the generator sees the
precondition, guard, and loop body but not the verification target \(Q\).
It produces ACSL invariants \(I\), and a result succeeds only when Frama-C/WP
proves both that \(I\) is inductive and that
\(I\wedge\neg B\Rightarrow Q\).
\sam{} trains the generator without querying \(Q\).  For supervised
fine-tuning, multiple target-hidden responses are combined, optionally
repaired, and filtered by Houdini; the verified invariant set becomes the
target paired with the masked program.  Reinforcement learning uses a separate
execution signal: reachable states and three conservatively filtered kinds of
synthetic negative examples.  Rewards measure which negatives are rejected by
the inductive clauses in each response, with an additional group-relative
bonus for less common contributions.  SFT data synthesis, RL, and inference
use the same candidate normalization, union, optional repair, and
Frama-C/WP-based Houdini filtering; only inference restores \(Q\) for the final
proof.  On 832 C programs, five-rollout inference verifies 74.76\% of tasks,
compared with 61.18\% for the strongest external baseline.  Reinforcement
learning raises combine@10 from 52.40\% to 56.97\% and reduces the rollouts
needed to reach 95\% of the measured maximum from 30 to 10.
\end{abstract}
