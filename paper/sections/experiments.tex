\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}
%% ============================================================

We ask: (1) how much union and Houdini improve target-hidden inference,
(2) whether RL improves combined inference rather than merely pass@1, and
(3) whether the gains reflect clause complementarity and a reliable reward.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp:setup}

\paragraph{Benchmarks.}
We evaluate 832 integer C programs.  The \emph{Core-366} subset
contains 316 linear programs from Code2Inv, SyGuS, and SV-COMP and 50
nonlinear programs from LIPuS.  The remaining 466 programs are the integer
fragment of Loopy's original 469-program corpus; three floating-point programs
are excluded because they fall outside our scalar-integer predicate language.
Unsupported inputs remain failures in the common denominator.

\paragraph{Target-hidden evaluation.}
Before any model-facing step, we remove \texttt{assert} and
\texttt{ensures} predicates, executable assertion calls, and conventional
error-label names.  Preconditions and executable loop semantics remain
visible.  After a method fixes its invariants, they are inserted into the
untouched original program.  A task succeeds only if the source parses,
the verifier proves establishment and preservation for every retained
invariant, and all restored target goals succeed.  Failures, timeouts,
malformed outputs, and unsupported programs count as zero.

\paragraph{Verifier, models, and baselines.}
All final checks use Frama-C 31.0 (Gallium)~\citep{framac}, WP, and Z3
4.13.3.
The main inference comparison fixes \texttt{gpt-5-nano} across all
\sam{} variants, so differences among those rows come only from inference.
We compare with the native target-hidden pipelines of \textsc{AutoSpec},
\textsc{SESpec}, and \textsc{Clause2Inv}, and with Daikon 5.8.24
~\citep{wen2024enchanting,yang2026sespec,cao2025clause2inv,daikon}.
\textsc{Clause2Inv} supports only the 366 programs with transition
verification conditions; its remaining inputs still count as failures.

The model-scale analysis samples 100 target-hidden responses per task from the
official Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-14B, and Qwen3-30B-A3B checkpoints
~\citep{yang2025qwen3}, at temperature 1.0 with thinking disabled.
The reinforcement-learning comparison uses Qwen3-8B and the corresponding
8B-RL-Zero checkpoint under the same 100-response protocol.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
For a program with \(c\) independently successful responses among \(n=100\),
direct pass@\(k\) uses the unbiased estimator
\(1-\binom{n-c}{k}/\binom{n}{k}\).
Combine@\(k\) instead unions the clauses from the first \(k\) responses,
applies Houdini, and tests the resulting invariant set against the restored
target.  Direct pass@\(k\) asks whether one response is independently
\(Q\)-sufficient; combine@\(k\) asks whether the accumulated, filtered union
is \(Q\)-sufficient and therefore measures deployment.  We summarize
compression of exploration with
\[
  k_{95}=\min\{k:C_\pi(k)\geq0.95\,C_\pi(100)\},
\]
where \(C_\pi(k)\) is combine@\(k\) for policy \(\pi\).

\subsection{RQ1: How Much Does Combined Inference Help?}
\label{sec:exp:e2e}

\sam{} (pass@1) tests one response from the \sam{} prompt as a standalone
invariant set.  \sam{} (combine@1), \sam{} (combine@5), and \sam{}
(combine@10) combine and filter clauses from one, five, or ten target-hidden
responses, respectively.  Retries are disabled in this comparison.

Five-response inference verifies 622 programs, 108 more than the strongest

Ten responses add only 16 proofs over five while roughly doubling tokens and
time.  This diminishing return motivates a model whose small union contains
clauses the base model reveals only under wider sampling.

\subsection{RQ2: Does RL Align the Model with Combined Inference?}
\label{sec:exp:rlzero}

We compare Qwen3-8B with 8B-RL-Zero while holding the candidate union,
Houdini filter, target-hiding protocol, and response budget fixed.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/qwen_rlzero_probe.pdf}
  \caption{Reinforcement learning improves combined inference even though it
  does not improve the chance that one response proves the target.  With ten
  responses, 8B-RL-Zero exceeds the base model's combine@100 result.}
  \label{fig:qwen-rlzero}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:qwen-rlzero} shows different trends for standalone
target-proving responses and complementary clauses.  RL-Zero lowers direct
pass@1 from 9.30\% to 4.88\%
and leaves direct pass@100 nearly unchanged (33.29\% versus 32.93\%).
Under the deployed combination procedure, however, combine@10 rises from
52.40\% to 56.97\%, and combine@100 rises from 55.41\% to 58.77\%.
Ten RL-Zero responses outperform 100 base-model responses, while
\(k_{95}\) falls from 30 to 10.

The pass@1 decrease is not a failure under the deployment objective: one
ten-response invocation exceeds the 100-response base result.  Coverage
strengthens each response, while the group bonus rewards behavior missed by
peers even when no response is independently \(Q\)-sufficient.

\subsection{RQ3: Where Does the Compositional Gain Come From?}
\label{sec:exp:probe}

\paragraph{Partial answers remain useful after filtering.}
Inductiveness by itself cannot distinguish a useful invariant from
\texttt{true}, and a binary whole-response reward discards useful clauses when
another clause in the same response fails.  Reinforcement learning with
verifiable rewards can also concentrate probability on already rewarded paths
while reducing large-\(k\) exploration~\citep{yue2025rlcapacity}.
Union and Houdini expose the value that these objectives miss: valid clauses
survive even when the response containing them does not prove the target on
its own.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/qwen_official_probe.pdf}
  \caption{Direct pass@\(k\) measures standalone target-proving responses,
  whereas combine@\(k\) measures whether the accumulated clauses contain a
  \(Q\)-sufficient Houdini-filtered subset.  For all four official Qwen3
  checkpoints, the
  combine curves change by at most 0.60 percentage points after \(k=30\).}
  \label{fig:qwen-probe}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:qwen-probe} shows that partial responses contain substantial
value.  Direct pass@100 is 15.62\% for Qwen3-4B, 33.29\% for 8B, and
39.90\% for 14B.  Combining clauses raises 4B to 47.84\% and 14B to
58.05\%.  Houdini can recover compatible clauses from imperfect responses, but
it cannot invent a relation that the model never emits.  The gap between direct
pass@\(k\) and combine@\(k\) is evidence that responses contain reusable
partial proofs rather than only all-or-nothing solutions.

No checkpoint gains more than 0.60 points from combine@30 to combine@100.
Reward must therefore strengthen early responses while preserving
complementarity, not rely on unbounded sampling.

\paragraph{Reward and negative-example checks.}
Across ten hand-written specifications, every gold invariant survives the
verifier filter and rejects at least 99.6\% of the intended negative examples,
with no ordering violations in the reward sanity checks.  A larger audit on
Core-366 compares 486,302 generated negative states against an expanded set of
program executions and finds no hard pair-level collision.  These checks do not
prove global unreachability, but support the conservative construction.  With
the opposite movement of pass@1 and combine@\(k\), they support interpreting
the reward as a signal for strong, complementary clauses rather than hidden-
target labels.
