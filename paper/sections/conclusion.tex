\section{Conclusion and Limitations}\label{sec:conclusion}
%% ============================================================

We studied loop-invariant generation when the assertion or postcondition is
hidden from the generator.  \sam{} uses the same verified candidate pipeline
to synthesize SFT pairs, train with execution-based rewards, and perform
inference.  SFT targets are Houdini-filtered invariant sets paired with masked
programs.  Separately, reachable executions and conservatively filtered
negative states provide the reinforcement-learning signal.  The original
target is restored only for final verification.

The experiments show that combining multiple target-hidden responses is
substantially more effective than relying on a single complete response, and
that reinforcement learning improves both the attainable combined verification
rate and response efficiency.  These results support evaluating learned
invariant generators under the candidate-combination procedure actually used
at deployment, rather than by standalone pass rate alone.

The current implementation is limited to one loop over scalar integer state.
Its synthetic negatives are conservatively checked training examples, not
proofs of global unreachability, and final soundness still depends on
Frama-C/WP.  Extending the approach to multiple loops, arrays, heaps, richer
assertion languages, and inter-loop properties remains future work
~\citep{liu2024towards}.
