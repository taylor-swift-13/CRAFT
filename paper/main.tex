\begin{abstract}
Valid loop invariants are closed under conjunction: useful clauses from
different responses can prove a target even when no response does so alone.
Yet standard training scores each response as an indivisible answer.  We
study this mismatch under \emph{target-hidden loop verification}, where target
assertions and postconditions \(\mathcal G\) are withheld during generation and
training.  CRAFT addresses this mismatch through two complementary operations:
decompose each rollout so that a partially incorrect response does not erase
its valid clauses, and compose verified clauses across rollouts so that
complementary partial answers can form a proof.  At inference, it realizes
this idea through rollout--decompose--pool--filter--compose: responses are
decomposed into clauses, pooled across rollouts, filtered with syntax checking
and Houdini, and composed into one invariant before restoring \(\mathcal G\).
Training
is aligned with inference through the same filtered clause pool:
rejection-filtered SFT distills accepted multi-response pools into single
responses, while RL scores each response by the negative coverage of its
pooled survivors and uses Shapley allocation to preserve complementary
exploration.  Both stages transfer the benefits of multi-response exploration
to fewer inference-time samples: SFT learns from composed exploration
outcomes, and RL further increases the probability of producing useful
clauses within a single rollout.
The only learned generator in both stages is the small open-weight model being
trained: SFT acceptance and RL rewards use only program executions and
verifier outcomes, without an external teacher or reward model.
On 832 C programs, the inference pipeline raises bare Qwen3-8B from 6.43\%
pass@1 to 37.86\% compose@1.  Large-scale SFT then reaches 63.90\%
compose@1.  A subsequent RL stage uses only 4,992 records---15.0\% as many
as SFT, with partial source overlap---yet raises compose@1 to 69.23\%.
This 5.33-point gain reduces verification error by 14.8\%.  The gain is
concentrated at the primary metric: compose@8--32 stays within 0.6 points of
the SFT checkpoint.  Thus, even after SFT has nearly saturated performance at
larger sampling budgets, RL further improves single-rollout generation.
Under the same target-hidden reasoning-off protocol, one
SFT+RL Qwen3-8B rollout exceeds Claude Sonnet-4.6 at compose@1 by 12.26
points.  Reward ablations show why these gains need not sacrifice
exploration: Shapley credit adds 9.85--11.30 compose points over
clause-decomposed credit, while binary and whole-response rewards collapse
composition.
\end{abstract}
