\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
%% ============================================================

Loop invariants turn unbounded executions into finite proofs, but validity
alone does not imply usefulness: \(\mathit{true}\) is valid for every loop and
proves nothing downstream.  At the same time, valid invariants are closed
under conjunction.  A range bound from one response and a relational equality
from another can therefore prove a target that neither response proves alone,
while clause-wise verification can discard failures without losing useful
clauses.  This makes the filtered cross-response clause pool, rather than one
complete response, the natural inference object.

Whole-response rewards are mismatched to this object.  They erase credit for
a useful clause when a sibling clause fails, and they value a complementary
constraint no more than one repeated by every peer.  More generally,
response-level RLVR can sharpen probability on already-supported answers
without expanding large-\(k\) coverage~\citep{yue2025rlcapacity}, potentially
reducing the diversity that composition needs.  Our central claim is therefore
an alignment claim: the training reward should value the same verified
composition that inference constructs from a fixed rollout group, rather than
each rollout as an independent final answer.

We study this claim under \emph{target-hidden loop verification}.  The model
sees the loop context but not the target assertions and postconditions \(\mathcal G\).
After the invariant set is fixed, the untouched source is restored and the
verifier checks both induction and every original target.  Hiding \(\mathcal G\)
prevents target copying and target-specific proof feedback, so generation and
training require a graded, target-independent strength signal.

At inference, \sam{} implements
\emph{rollout--decompose--pool--filter--compose}: given \(k\) responses, it
extracts and pools clauses, filters them with syntax checks and Houdini, and
then restores \(\mathcal G\).  compose@\(k\) measures the verified rate of this composed
prefix, preserving partial answers that pass@\(k\) discards; compose@1 is
our primary optimization and reporting metric.  Training is
aligned to this object at the same two compositional levels.  Within a rollout,
execution-derived negative coverage rewards each response according to the
clauses retained by the pooled inference filter; across each GRPO group~\citep{shao2024deepseekmath}, a
closed-form Shapley term discounts coverage repeated by multiple peers.  SFT
distills the same filtered multi-response union into one response.  The two
training stages therefore have distinct roles: SFT raises the compositional
ceiling, RL amortizes that capability into the first rollout, and Shapley
credit enables this compression without collapsing complementary
exploration.  Both stages are self-generated: the only learned generator is
the small open-weight model being trained, while the program executor and
verifier provide every SFT acceptance decision and RL reward.  No external
teacher model generates targets, critiques responses, or supplies reward.

\tightparagraph{Contributions.}
This paper makes three contributions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item We introduce a rollout--decompose--pool--filter--compose inference
  framework for target-hidden invariant generation and use compose@1 as its
  primary metric.  On Qwen3-8B, the framework raises one-response
  verification from 6.43\% pass@1 to 37.86\% compose@1, a 31.43-point gain.
  \item We introduce a two-stage training pipeline: rejection-filtered SFT
  raises Qwen3-8B compose@1 to 65.50\%, and a subsequent 4,992-record
  compositional RL stage reaches 69.23\%.  RL adds 3.73 points, reduces
  verification error by 10.8\%, and produces an 8B model that exceeds Claude
  Sonnet-4.6 at compose@1 by 12.26 points.  Target synthesis and
  reward computation require only the trained open-weight model, program
  execution, and verification, with no external teacher model.
  \item Through controlled reward and sampler ablations, cross-model
  evaluation, and 832-program analysis, we show that decompose--compose RLVR
  provides effective credit for invariant generation.  Clause-decomposed
  feedback preserves partially correct responses, while Shapley allocation
  adds 9.85--11.30 compose points over clause-only credit and mitigates the
  exploration collapse of binary and whole-response rewards.
\end{itemize}
