\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
%% ============================================================

\paragraph{Candidate generation, filtering, and prompt-time composition.}
Classical invariant synthesis uses abstract interpretation, affine relations,
templates, interpolation, recurrences, syntax-guided search, or
counterexamples; execution-driven systems instead mine likely properties from
traces~\citep{cousot1977abstract,karr1976affine,colon2003linear,
mcmillan2003interpolation,kincaid2017compositional,alur2013sygus,
garg2014ice,daikon}.  Houdini iteratively removes invalid candidates to retain
an inductive subset~\citep{flanagan2001houdini}.  LLM workflows reuse this
pattern: \textsc{Loopy} couples proposals with Houdini and repair,
\textsc{iRank} ranks candidates, and \textsc{Clause2Inv} composes retained
clauses while iteratively accumulating counterexamples
~\citep{kamath2023finding,chakraborty2023ranking,cao2025clause2inv}.
\textsc{LaM4Inv}, \textsc{ACInv}, \textsc{AutoSpec}, \textsc{SpecGen},
\textsc{SESpec}, \textsc{LEMUR}, and \textsc{Baldur} similarly organize
generation with static analysis, symbolic execution, verification, or repair
~\citep{wu2024lam4inv,liu2024acinv,wen2024enchanting,ma2024specgen,
yang2026sespec,wu2024lemur,first2023baldur}.  These are inference workflows;
\sam{} fixes all target-hidden rollouts before joint filtering, receives no
verifier feedback between calls, and trains responses for fixed-group
composition.

\paragraph{Specialized neural invariant learning.}
\textsc{Code2Inv} learns a construction policy from solver feedback;
\textsc{CLN2INV} and G-CLN learn continuous representations of linear and
nonlinear relations; and \textsc{LIPuS} composes RL-based pruning with SMT
solving~\citep{si2018learning,si2020code2inv,ryan2020cln2inv,
yao2020gcln,yu2023lipus}.  These task-specific models optimize one candidate
or construction trajectory.  We instead post-train a general language model
so independently sampled responses contribute jointly under a fixed budget.

\paragraph{Behavioral-example objectives for specification strength.}
\textsc{COINS} assesses specifications using trusted positive and
mutation-derived negative input--output cases~\citep{yang2026coins};
\textsc{SpecRL} rewards complete method-level specifications that reject
impossible input--output pairs~\citep{huang2026specrl}; it neither targets
loop invariants nor derives negatives from loop dynamics.  Both show that
behavioral examples distinguish weak but valid specifications from stronger
ones.  \sam{} supplies that loop-specific construction, filters each imperfect
response to an inductive clause subset, and conditions its credit on negative
states already rejected by peer rollouts.

\paragraph{Language-model training from formal feedback.}
\textsc{InvBench} and Wonda use verified data for invariant fine-tuning;
\textsc{CodeRL} uses execution signals; and \textsc{Re:Form} applies
verifier-guided RL to individual Dafny proofs
~\citep{wei2025invbench,pinto2026notall,le2022coderl,yan2025reform}.
Unlike whole-output verifier rewards, \sam{} trains toward a filtered clause
pool by salvaging the inductive part of an imperfect response and rewarding
its strength and complementary group coverage.
This distinction matters because verifiable-reward RL can improve small-budget
accuracy without expanding broad-sampling coverage~\citep{yue2025rlcapacity}.
