\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
%% ============================================================

Loop invariants are properties that hold at loop entry and are preserved by
iteration.  By summarizing states reachable after arbitrarily many iterations,
they reduce unbounded execution to finite proof obligations and are central
to verifying program safety and functional correctness.  Yet generating
sufficiently strong invariants remains difficult: a response may mix useful
clauses with incorrect ones, or lack complementary constraints found in
another response.  A failed response can therefore contain useful proof
material.  Emitting more candidate clauses may lower whole-response success
(pass) while expanding the candidates available for filtering and composition
(compose).  Our objective is the success of the filtered composition, without
requiring each response to succeed independently.

We study \emph{target-hidden loop verification}: the model sees the loop
context, while target assertions and postconditions \(\mathcal G\) are withheld.
This prevents target copying and removes target-specific proof feedback from
generation and training.  Inductive validity alone is insufficient: a valid
but weak invariant may fail to prove the hidden targets.  Training therefore
needs a target-independent strength signal beyond validity.

We introduce \sam{} to align compositional inference, supervision targets,
and RL rewards.  At inference, it decomposes responses into clauses, pools
and jointly filters them, and composes the survivors.  During training,
execution-derived negative-trace coverage supplies a target-independent
strength signal.  SFT learns multi-response compositions accepted by
verification and coverage screening; RL rewards the coverage of clauses
retained by the same filtering procedure, with Shapley credit allocating
shared coverage among contributing responses.  Useful parts can thus receive
credit even when the complete response fails.  The two stages aim to make
multi-response discoveries accessible with fewer samples.  All supervision
comes from the model's own generations, program execution, and verification,
without an external teacher or reward model.

Evaluation on 832 C programs shows that compositional inference substantially
improves bare-model verification and that aligned training yields further
gains.  We use compose@1, the verification rate after filtering one response,
as our primary metric.

\tightparagraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item We introduce a compositional inference framework for target-hidden
  invariant generation.  Pooling clauses before Houdini filtering preserves
  useful parts of imperfect responses and combines complementary constraints.
  With one response, bare Qwen3-8B reaches 37.86\% compose@1 versus
  6.43\% pass@1.
  \item We align SFT targets and RL credit with compositional inference.
  SFT distills accepted multi-response compositions into single-response
  supervision, raising Qwen3-8B compose@1 to 63.90\%.  RL rewards surviving
  clauses' coverage and group contributions even when the complete response
  fails, further raising compose@1 to 69.23\%.
  \item Our experiments reveal that whole-response success and compositional
  verification can diverge.  SFT broadens verification coverage and compresses
  the sampling budget; after substantial SFT, RL mainly improves sampling
  efficiency at small budgets, with little change in coverage at the largest
  tested budget.
\end{itemize}
