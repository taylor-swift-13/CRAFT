%% ============================================================
\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
%% ============================================================

CRAFT aligns training with compositional inference.  At inference, it splits
responses into clauses, pools clauses across responses, verifier-filters the
pool, and conjoins the survivors.  SFT distills this pooled result into one
response, while RL credits the verified clauses that contribute
target-independent strength.  Assertions and postconditions remain hidden
throughout generation and training and are restored only for final
verification.  Appendix~\ref{app:formal-method} gives the complete formal
definition.

\subsection{Target-Hidden Composable Inference}
\label{sec:method:core}
\label{sec:method:inference}

For a masked loop \(\mathcal L\), let \(\operatorname{parts}(y)\) be the
invariant clauses extracted from response \(y\).  Algorithm~\ref{alg:method-pipeline}
defines the complete inference procedure.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Rollout--decompose--pool--filter--compose}
\label{alg:method-pipeline}
\begin{algorithmic}
\alginput{task \(\mathcal T=(\mathcal L,\mathcal G)\), policy \(\pi\), number of responses \(n\)}
\algoutput{composed invariant \(I\), final verdict \(v\)}
\algline \(x\leftarrow\mathcal L\) by masking \(\mathcal G\) in \(\mathcal T\)\cmnt{hide target}\\
\algline \(y_{1:n}\sim\pi(\cdot\mid x)\)\cmnt{rollout}\\
\algline \(A_i\leftarrow\operatorname{parts}(y_i)\) for \(i=1,\ldots,n\)
  \cmnt{decompose}\\
\algline \(P\leftarrow\operatorname{Unique}(\bigcup_i A_i)\)\cmnt{pool}\\
\algline \(C\leftarrow\operatorname{Filter}_{\mathcal L}(P)\)\cmnt{filter}\\
\algline \(I\leftarrow\bigwedge_{c\in C}c\)\cmnt{compose}\\
\algline \(v\leftarrow\operatorname{TaskVerify}(\mathcal T,C)\)\cmnt{restore target}\\
\algline \kw{return} \((I,v)\)\\
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\(\operatorname{Unique}\) performs conservative duplicate removal, and
\(\operatorname{Filter}\) is resource-bounded Houdini: establishment and
preservation are checked clause by clause under the current candidate family,
so clauses from different responses may support one another.  Timeout and
unknown cause deletion; a set is returned only after all its clauses are
proved in their final joint context, while hitting the round limit returns
the conservative empty result.  Appendix~\ref{app:formal-loop-verification} separates the semantic
definition of an invariant from the Frama-C-aligned loop-verifier interface;
Appendix~\ref{app:formal-houdini} defines both ideal and bounded Houdini.
All clause statuses in one round may use the pre-deletion candidate context,
including hypotheses whose own goals fail.  Houdini therefore reruns the
survivors after every deletion and stops only when one complete round deletes
nothing.

Only after \(I\) is fixed do we restore the hidden targets \(\mathcal G\)
and run the final verification conditions at their original locations.  The
inference objective is therefore
\[
  \max_\pi\ \Pr_{y_{1:n}\sim\pi(\cdot\mid\mathcal L)}
  [\operatorname{TaskVerify}((\mathcal L,\mathcal G),C(y_{1:n}))].
\]
Its empirical estimate is compose@\(n\).  It can exceed pass@\(n\) because a
conjunction of valid invariants remains valid and may combine complementary
bounds and relations.  Appendix~\ref{app:formal-method} gives the complete
definitions and algorithms; Appendix~\ref{app:composition-proofs} states and
proves the composition and filtering properties.

\subsection{Target-Independent Strength Signal}
\label{sec:method:sampler}

Because targets are hidden, training cannot score direct target proofs.  We
instead execute the masked loop to collect reachable loop-head states
\(\mathcal E\) and construct target-independent negative traces
\(\mathcal N\).  The traces are either local relational perturbations or
continuations beyond a witnessed loop exit.  Conservative cleaning removes
observed reachable states, possible fresh entries, duplicates, and unsupported
states; Appendix~\ref{app:negative-sampling} gives the construction.

For a verified clause set \(C\), let \(\operatorname{rej}(C)\) contain every
negative trace on which some clause is false.  Its trajectory-level coverage
is
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{cov}(C)=
  \frac{|\operatorname{rej}(C)|}{|\mathcal N|},
  \qquad |\mathcal N|>0.
  \label{eq:method-coverage}
\end{equation}
Each trace contributes one unit regardless of its length.  Instances with no
retained negatives are not used for SFT acceptance or RL updates.

\subsection{Distilling Multi-Response Search}
\label{sec:method:sft}

SFT uses Algorithm~\ref{alg:method-pipeline} as a target synthesizer.  Starting
from an empty raw pool, each round adds a new group of base-model clauses and
re-filters the entire accumulated pool:
\begin{equation}
  P_t=P_{t-1}\cup\bigcup_i\operatorname{parts}(y_i^{(t)}),
  \qquad
  C_t=\operatorname{Filter}_{\mathcal L}(\operatorname{Unique}(P_t)).
  \label{eq:method-sft}
\end{equation}
Re-filtering the raw pool allows a previously removed clause to return when a
later response supplies a supporting clause.  The first set satisfying
\(\operatorname{cov}(C_t)\ge\tau\) is serialized as one SFT target, where
\(\tau\) is the acceptance threshold; otherwise the instance is dropped after
the synthesis budget is exhausted.  Neither the SFT input nor its target
contains \(\mathcal G\).

\subsection{Clause and Group Credit}
\label{sec:method:reward}

RL applies exactly the same pool-then-filter order once per response group.
Let \(L_i=\operatorname{parts}(y_i)\),
\(o_i=\max(0,|L_i|-m_{\max})\), and
\(A_i=\operatorname{Unique}(\operatorname{First}_{m_{\max}}(L_i))\).  Thus
\(\operatorname{First}_{m}(L)\) is the prefix containing at most \(m\) clauses,
\(o_i\) counts clauses beyond the per-response cap, while \(A_i\) retains the
admitted clauses and their provenance.  The clauses are then de-duplicated
globally and filtered only after the full group has been pooled:
\begin{align}
  P^*&=\operatorname{Unique}\!\left(\bigcup_iA_i\right),
  &C^*&=\operatorname{Filter}_{\mathcal L}(P^*),\\
  V_i&=\{c\in A_i:\operatorname{key}(c)\in\operatorname{key}(C^*)\},
  &R_i&=\operatorname{rej}(V_i).
  \label{eq:method-credit-pool}
\end{align}
Thus \(V_i\) assigns each pooled survivor back to every response that proposed
it.  A useful clause can earn credit even when other clauses in its response
fail, while mutually supporting clauses are checked in the same pooled context
used at inference.  The cheap reachable-state check is an internal front-end
of this single pooled filtering call; it does not filter responses separately.

Coverage alone gives the same score to a unique discovery and a clause already
supplied by every peer.  Let
\(f(\nu)=|\{j:\nu\in R_j\}|\).  We add the closed-form Shapley allocation of
the fixed coverage game:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:shapley-credit}
  \phi_i=\frac{1}{|\mathcal N|}
  \sum_{\nu\in R_i}\frac{1}{f(\nu)}.
\end{equation}
The rollout reward is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:generation-reward}
  r_i=w_c\frac{|R_i|}{|\mathcal N|}+w_g\phi_i-w_o o_i.
\end{equation}
Here \(w_c\), \(w_g\), and \(w_o\) denote the coverage, group-credit, and
overflow weights.  The first term is the main verified-coverage reward; the
second is a complementarity bonus that divides redundant coverage among its
proposers; the final term penalizes clauses emitted beyond the response cap.
It is the Shapley value after filtering the full group, not the value of a game
that reruns Houdini for every coalition.  A clause that only supports another
clause receives no direct coverage credit.

GRPO normalizes these rewards within the sampled group and applies the
standard clipped policy update (Appendix~\ref{app:grpo-details}).  Training and
inference are aligned because inference, SFT synthesis, and RL scoring all use
the same operation—filtering the union of decomposed responses—while the
reward remains independent of the hidden target.
